# Basemap styles

A default basemap arrives full of colour that is not our data: pink hospitals,
yellow shops, brown transit lines, and a POI pin for every business in the
country. Our own pins have to compete with all of it, which is backwards for a
map whose whole job is "where can I use this card".

`public/map/light.json` and `public/map/dark.json` strip it back to the three
things a reader needs to locate themselves: the coastline, the road network,
and the names of towns. Everything else is off. The result is that **the only
colour on the map is our dots**, which is what makes the fighter blue and the
voucher amber readable at 10px.

They are MapLibre style documents drawn on
[OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/) vector tiles: no API key, no account,
no billing, no per-load charge. `MapView.tsx` picks the file that matches the
reader's colour scheme and swaps it live when the system theme changes.

## What each rule is doing

- **No POI or transit layers at all.** Somebody else's business pins are not
  our data, and a reader who taps one gets their info window instead of ours.
- **No road labels.** Street names are noise at the zoom levels this map lives
  at, and the last fifty metres is Waze's job, which is why every place carries
  a Waze deep link.
- **Town names kept, at full ink.** "Is that dot near Ashdod" is the question
  the basemap exists to answer, so `place` labels are the one thing that stays
  loud. They prefer `name:he` and fall back to `name`.
- **Landscape is the page background, water one step darker.** The coastline
  reads as a shape rather than a blue field, so it orients without pulling the
  eye.
- **Country border as a hairline, provinces off.**

## Editing them

They are generated by hand, not by a tool, so edit the JSON directly. The
source is `openmaptiles` at `https://tiles.openfreemap.org/planet` and the
schema is [OpenMapTiles](https://openmaptiles.org/schema/), so any layer in
that schema is available. Fonts come from OpenFreeMap's glyph endpoint and
"Noto Sans Regular" and "Noto Sans Bold" both cover Hebrew.

After changing a colour, run `scripts/check_palette.py`. The two benefit
colours have to stay distinguishable under protanopia, deuteranopia and
tritanopia, and the basemap sits directly behind them.
